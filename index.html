📖 What is this & how does it work? ▲

The idea

This simulator answers a classic investing debate: Is it better to invest a fixed amount every month (SIP), or wait patiently for market crashes and invest a lump sum at the bottom?

We pit two characters against each other — both with the same income and the same savings rate, over the same time period:

🙂
Aam Aadmi

Invests a fixed amount every single month via SIP — rain, shine, crash or boom. No timing, no second-guessing.

🧞
The Genie

Has perfect foresight of market bottoms. Parks all savings in idle cash, then deploys everything at once when the market falls enough.

Key assumptions

📊 Market data Equity returns use Nifty 50 annual returns (fetched live from Yahoo Finance; falls back to historical data through 2024). Gold uses GOLDBEES ETF (INR-denominated, from 2007; estimated prior). Debt uses indicative FD/gilt rates.
💰 Parity Both characters invest the exact same rupee amount from their salary each month. The Genie doesn't invest more — he just holds it as cash and deploys it later. The comparison is purely about timing, not amount.
🏦 Idle cash The Genie's uninvested cash earns a fixed annual rate (default 6.5% p.a., like a liquid fund or FD). This interest also gets deployed at the next bottom, giving the Genie a small edge — but it's realistic since idle money doesn't sit under a mattress.
📉 Bottom detection A "bottom" is defined as a point where equity has fallen X% from its recent peak (you set X). A cooldown period prevents multiple triggers in quick succession. This is the Genie's superpower — in reality, no one can identify bottoms in real time.
📈 Returns model Annual returns are applied uniformly across all 12 months of each year (monthly compounding). This smooths intra-year volatility. Real markets are lumpier — the simulation is directionally accurate, not tick-by-tick precise.
🪙 Asset allocation Both characters use the same equity/gold/debt mix that you set. The Genie only times his equity deployment — the blended return applies once he invests. No rebalancing is modelled.
📅 Income step-up Both characters increase their monthly investment by the same % each year (matching salary growth). This keeps the comparison fair even as incomes rise over time.
🏷️ Taxes & costs No taxes (LTCG, STCG, dividend tax), no expense ratios, no transaction costs, no exit loads are modelled. Real returns will be lower. The comparison is pre-tax and pre-cost for both equally.
📐 Inflation adjustment When the "real returns" toggle is on, corpus values are deflated by estimated Indian CPI (sourced from historical averages). This shows purchasing power, not nominal value.

What this is not

This is not financial advice. The Genie is a hypothetical construct — perfect market timing is impossible in practice. The simulation is a thought experiment to help you understand how much edge market timing would need to have to beat consistent SIPs, and whether the answer surprises you.
